(a) ObjNav (Phase 1): with privileged object size information, the policy learns load-aware navigation strategies, adapting its behavior across sparse and narrow spaces according to different object geometries (X < Y, X = Y, X > Y); (b) PreObjNav (Phase 2): the robot infers object geometry from proprioception over time, enabling adaptive navigation without size observation.

Abstract

Navigating cluttered environments while carrying objects remains a challenging problem for quadruped robots. Compared with extensive works that focused on load-aware locomotion adaptation to maintain gait stability, existing autonomous navigation systems generally ignore the influence of carried objects, which may extend the robot's effective geometric boundary, leading to additional collisions or causing the robot to become stuck during navigation. To address this, we propose a two-phase load-aware navigation learning framework. In Phase 1, we train ObjNav, a load-aware navigation policy via reinforcement learning, enabling the robot to adapt its trajectory and clearance behavior according to the geometric size of the carried object. In Phase 2, we train PreObjNav, an online geometry predictor via imitation learning, which estimates unknown payload geometry from interaction-based collision feedback. We evaluate the framework in cluttered environments with loads of varying sizes and shapes. The results show that our navigation policy exhibits clear load-aware behaviors, and the online geometry predictor effectively estimates object geometry through slight collision interactions, achieving comparable navigation performance to a given-geometry policy without requiring prior knowledge of the carried load. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
